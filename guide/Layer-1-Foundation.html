What a CRM actually is

A system which tracks your interactions with other people. That is the whole of it.

Everything in these 8 guides is a consequence of that one sentence. It is a record of who you know and what has passed between you, kept somewhere it will still be readable years from now. It is not a database you query, and not a sales tool you have to feed.

So why does almost nobody use theirs

Ask anyone who has abandoned a CRM and you get the same answer twice: it was too complicated, and it became admin. Those are the same answer. A system that asks you to type records in competes with everything else in your day, and it loses, usually within 1 or 2 months. Then the records go stale, then you stop trusting them, then you stop opening it.

So every decision in this build is made against admin. If a step exists only to keep the system fed, it does not go in.

Why this is built in layers

The instinct is to plan the whole system and then build it in order, front to back. That is how school works and how most projects are run, so it gets applied here without much thought. It does not work for a system like this, because you cannot know what the finished system needs until parts of it exist.

Layers work differently. Each one solves one problem and is useful on its own. Nothing is thrown away when the next arrives; it gets built on. Think of a game character rather than a road: abilities accumulate, and a character at level 40 still has everything they had at level 10.

What you will own at the end

A system that keeps a record of everyone you deal with, builds its own history from sources you already have, tells you each morning who to speak to and why, refuses to contact anyone without you, and reports when it stops working.

It lives on your own machine and the records are yours outright. The only running cost anywhere in the build is the AI model in Layer 5, and you choose which one and how much of it you use. Layers 1 to 4 and 6 to 8 use no AI at all.

The one rule for building it

In order, one layer at a time, and do not move on until the one you are on works. The temptation is to jump ahead to the assistants in Layer 5, because that is the interesting part. Assistants standing on a foundation that cannot reliably say who someone is will produce confident, well-written, wrong answers all day.

There is no schedule. Move to the next layer when you have finished the one you are on, whether that is tonight or next month.

Layer 1 · Foundation

Where it all lives

Your CRM is a folder of plain text files on your own computer. That is the actual design, not a simplified version of it for teaching.

Why not a hosted CRM product

A hosted product keeps your records on someone else's computers, in their format, and you get them back on their terms. There is a second reason that matters more here: later layers need software to read and write these records directly. A screen built for human clicking gives software no orderly way in.

Why plain text

A text file opens on any machine, in any decade, with or without the app you made it in. You will use Obsidian to look at these files, because it is free and it shows how they link together. But Obsidian only reads the folder. If it vanished tomorrow, every record would be exactly where you left it.

Where it goes, and where it does not

Your CRM is a vault of its own, separate from the second brain you have already built. 2 folders side by side, both yours, and Obsidian opens as many vaults as you like. What it must not be is a folder inside your second brain. Every layer after this one reads every file under the folder you name, and your second brain contains a great deal that has no business sitting in a contact record. The installer offers you a new folder called CRM; take it unless you have a reason not to.

The one rule that starts today

One person, one file. Never 2. The moment someone exists twice you have 2 half-histories and no way to know which is right. Layer 2 exists to protect this rule with actual machinery, but it begins with you not making a second file.

Layer 1 · Before you start

Before you start on a Mac

Everything in this guide is typed into Terminal, the Mac's window for typing commands instead of clicking. Press Command and Space together, type Terminal, and press Return.

Python

Install Python from python.org (the downloads page for macOS, the link labelled "macOS installer"; we tested 3.14.7). When it finishes, double-click Install Certificates.command and Update Shell Profile.command in the Python folder inside Applications, then open a new Terminal window and type:

python3 -c "import sys; print(sys.prefix)"

It should print a line starting /Library/Frameworks/Python.framework. If it starts /opt/homebrew or /usr/local/Cellar, your Terminal uses Homebrew's Python (Homebrew is an add-on installer many Mac owners use). The CRM works with either: it needs no add-ons, only Python itself.

Git

Git, the free tool that copies the code onto your Mac, comes with Apple's developer tools. The first time you type a line starting git, your Mac may show a box asking to install them. Press Install, wait until it has finished, then type the line again.

How to type the lines

Where this guide shows several lines in a black box, type them one at a time: type the first, press Return, wait for it to finish and give you a fresh line, then type the next. The installer asks you questions. Each offers an answer in [square brackets]; press Return to take it, or type your own and press Return.

Apple Silicon or Intel (the 2 kinds of chip a Mac can have; the Apple menu, then About This Mac, shows yours): the steps are the same on both, and both were tested.

Tried only on test Macs. Every step in this guide and in the instructions file of the layer's download was tried on test Macs (Macs GitHub rents out by the minute to run a script, not a person's own Mac, and not yours), never on a real Mac, except 2: the box asking to install the developer tools, which cannot appear on a test Mac; cd ~/Downloads/outliers-crm-01-foundation-mac-main, because it is only for the zip route, which was not tried on the test Macs. The last pages list every line and what happened to it.

Layer 1 · Finishing

What to do now

Nothing yet, and that is deliberate.

There is no data in your CRM yet, so anything this guide asked you to do at this point would be typing records in by hand. Typing is what kills CRMs, and starting that habit here would teach exactly the wrong lesson.

If you brought in a contacts export, open the folder in Obsidian and have a look at what came in. You will notice some people appear twice, some records are half empty, and some names are odd. That is normal. That is what most contact lists look like underneath. Layer 2 is what sorts it out, and seeing the mess first is the best possible reason to build it.

If you already had something like this

Some of you will have built something similar already. Here is your test rather than an instruction: find a person who exists twice in your system and work out which of the 2 records is right. If you cannot do that quickly and confidently, Layer 2 is doing real work for you and you should not skip it.

What this layer leaves unsolved

A folder will accept anything you put in it. It does not stop the same person going in twice under 2 spellings of their name, it does not check that a record has what it needs, and it has no way of knowing that "Rowan Ashdown", "R. Ashdown" and a profile link are one person.

Until that is fixed, nothing built on top can be trusted, because every clever feature you add later has to start by asking "who is this?" and getting the right answer.
